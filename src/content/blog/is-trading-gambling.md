Trading and gambling can look identical from the outside: money is put at risk on an uncertain outcome, and the result is either a gain or a loss. The meaningful difference is not the activity itself, it is what is behind the decision.

## What separates the two

Gambling, in the strict sense, involves a negative expected outcome over time, the house edge in a casino game is built into the game itself, and no amount of skill changes that over a large enough sample. Trading, done with a defined edge and controlled risk, does not have that built-in disadvantage. Whether a specific approach to trading behaves more like the first case or the second depends entirely on the process behind it, not on the fact that money is at risk.

## When trading starts to resemble gambling

Certain patterns push trading closer to gambling regardless of the market being traded:

- Position sizes large enough that a single loss meaningfully damages the account.
- Entries with no consistent, repeatable criteria behind them, chasing whatever looks exciting in the moment.
- No process for reviewing whether the approach actually works over time, only a focus on the outcome of the most recent trade.

Any of these, on their own, can turn an otherwise reasonable strategy into something that behaves like a bet rather than a calculated risk.

## What keeps trading on the other side of the line

Defined risk per trade, consistent entry criteria applied the same way regardless of how a trade feels, and a habit of reviewing results over a large sample rather than a handful of recent trades are what separate a repeatable process from a series of bets. The presence of risk is not what makes trading gambling. The absence of a process for managing that risk is.

## How to check where you actually stand

The most honest test is not a feeling, it is the data. Reviewing position size relative to account size, and the actual win rate and average result across a meaningful number of trades, shows whether an approach has a real edge or whether it is closer to guessing with extra steps.
